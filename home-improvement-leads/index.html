The First Hour

Why Speed Decides Who Gets the Job

A Harvard Business Review study of online sales leads found that companies who tried to reach a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to have a real conversation with the decision maker as those who waited even one hour longer. The same study found 23% of companies never responded at all.

Now picture that race on a shared lead. Three contractors get the same homeowner at 2:15 PM. One is on a roof, one is driving between bids, and one has an office manager who calls back tomorrow. The homeowner books whoever picks up, and that contractor usually didn't win on quality. They won on timing.

That's the part we take off your plate. When a homeowner applies, text and email follow-up starts right away, the booking calendar is in front of them, and reminders go out before the appointment. FAQ

Home Improvement Leads: Common Questions

What are home improvement leads?

A home improvement lead is a homeowner who has asked about a project, like a deck, a roof, new windows or a kitchen remodel, and left their contact details. Most lead sellers hand that name and number to several contractors at once. We book the homeowner onto your calendar instead, and nobody else gets them.

Are home improvement lead lists worth buying?

For high-ticket work, rarely. A list is a spreadsheet of homeowners who never asked to hear from you, so every name starts as a cold call. Homeowners who raised their hand for a specific project this month are worth far more than thousands who didn't.
