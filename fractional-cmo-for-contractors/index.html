The Engagement

How Hiring a Fractional CMO With Us Works

It runs like a marketing department, because it is one:

  1. Audit everything you already pay for. Every channel, every vendor, every dollar, and what each one actually produced in closed jobs.
  2. Set the plan and the budget. Where the money goes this quarter, what gets cut, and what gets tested next.
  3. Fix the leaks first. Tracking, the website, your Business Profile, speed-to-lead. There's no point pouring more in until the bucket holds.
  4. Run the appointment engine. Paid ads with you on camera, a qualifying application and booking, built inside your own accounts.
  5. Work the compounding channels. Reviews, SEO and AI search, past customers and referrals. Slower to start, cheaper every month after.
  6. Report and re-plan. A monthly report and review call, and a quarterly game plan with Spencer and team.

Who owns what

You own your ad accounts and your CRM data from day one, so you're never held hostage. Everything is built inside your own accounts, so nobody can hold a login over you.

Who you work with

Spencer and team, directly, on a shared board with regular calls. The person setting your strategy is the person accountable for it.

Choosing a Partner

Fractional CMO vs Agency vs Full-Time Hire

Three ways to get marketing leadership, and what each one leaves on your plate.

Questions to ask any fractional CMO before you hire

  1. Have they marketed a company like yours? Selling a $40K project at the kitchen table is not selling software.
  2. Who does the work? If the answer is "your existing vendors", you're paying for a manager, not a marketing department.
  3. What will they report every month? Closed jobs by channel, or impressions and "brand awareness"?
  4. Do you own every account? Ad accounts, website, data. In your name, in writing.
  5. Can you see named results? From contractors, with real numbers.

The same warning signs apply as with any agency. See contractor marketing agency red flags.

FAQ

Fractional CMO for Contractors: Common Questions

What does a fractional CMO do for a contractor?

They own your whole marketing number, the way a full-time head of marketing would. That means deciding where the budget goes, running every channel, tying each closed job back to where it came from, and reporting it to you every month. Here, the same team also does the work: ads, website, reviews, SEO, follow-up and past-customer campaigns.

How much do fractional CMO services cost?

We don't publish a price, because the scope changes with your size, your trade and how many channels you run. You get the real number on a 30-minute strategy call, plus a straight answer on whether a fractional CMO is what you need, or whether more appointments alone would do it.

How is a fractional CMO different from a marketing agency?

An agency is usually hired for one channel and measured on that channel. A fractional CMO is accountable for all of them, including the agencies. Here you get both: the person who sets the plan and the team that runs it, so there's no one left to blame but us.

Do I need a fractional CMO or just more appointments?

If your calendar is the problem and the rest of your marketing is fine, you need appointments, and the More Jobs System on its own is the better fit. A fractional CMO makes sense when you're past about $2M, you pay several vendors, and nobody owns the whole picture.

Does the 100 in 100 guarantee apply?

It applies when your fractional CMO scope includes running the paid appointment system: 100 exclusive appointment opportunities in 100 days, or you get paid three ways: a 100% refund, a $2,000 check from us, and we keep working for free until we hit 100. Who owns the ad accounts and the data?

You own your ad accounts and your CRM data from day one, so you're never held hostage. Everything gets built inside your own accounts.

What's not included?

Selling, estimating, project management, production, bookkeeping, hiring and day-to-day CRM administration. You run the jobs. We fill the calendar at a pace you can handle and show where every dollar came from.

Who is this not for?

Companies under about $2M a year, owners still doing the labor themselves, handymen and small repair work. If you're between $500K and $2M with crews, the appointment system on its own is usually the better first step, and we'll tell you so on the call.
