How It Works

How We Run Your Google Ads, Week by Week

  1. Check the market first. How many people in your service area search for what you sell. If the answer is "not many", we say so before you spend a dollar on it.
  2. Build it paused. Campaigns, keywords, the negative list, the ads and two to four landing pages, with tracking on every call and form. You review it all before it goes live.
  3. Launch and clean daily. The first weeks are mostly search-terms cleanup. Every search that was never a buyer becomes a negative keyword.
  4. Add Local Services Ads when you're ready. They work best once you have real reviews and someone picking up the phone live during the day.
  5. Judge it on jobs. A weekly check-in on cost per lead and cost per job. The account usually takes 30 to 60 days to settle, so that's the window to judge it on.

The part that decides whether any of it works

How fast someone calls the lead back. A search lead that waits until tomorrow has already booked the competitor who called back in minutes. If you run Google Ads alongside our appointment system, Google leads get the same instant follow-up, qualifying and booking onto your calendar.

Who owns what

You own your ad accounts and your CRM data from day one, so you're never held hostage. The account, its history and everything it learned stay in your name.

Who you work with

Spencer and team, directly. The person looking at your search terms is the person you talk to.

Questions to ask any Google Ads agency for contractors

  1. Whose name is the ad account in? If it's theirs, the history leaves when they do.
  2. Will they show you the search terms report? Not the keywords. The actual searches you paid for.
  3. What do they report every week? Cost per job, or clicks and impressions?
  4. Is it built paused for your review? You should see it before it spends.
  5. Who calls the leads back, and how fast? The best campaign in the country can't fix a callback that happens tomorrow.

FAQ

Google Ads Management for Contractors: Common Questions

What does Google Ads management for contractors include?

Account setup, campaign structure, keywords, negative keywords and ads, all built paused so you review it before a dollar is spent. Local Services Ads alongside search, two to four landing pages and conversion tracking. Then a daily search-terms review in the first weeks, and a weekly check-in on cost per lead and cost per job.

How much does Google Ads management cost?

We don't publish a price, because it depends on your trade, your market and whether Google Ads runs on its own or alongside the appointment system. You get the real number on a 30-minute strategy call, plus a straight answer on whether search should be your first channel at all.

Do you run Google Local Services Ads for contractors?

Yes, alongside search. Local Services Ads charge per lead instead of per click and sit at the top of the page. They work best when you have real review volume and someone answers the phone live during the day, because the homeowner can contact several companies from the same screen and the fastest call usually wins.

Should a contractor run Google Ads or social ads?

Search catches demand that already exists. Social ads create it. In a big market with steady searches for your service, search can carry real volume. In a small market it runs out of searches fast, and that's where the appointment system usually goes first. Most of what we run is paid social, so we'll tell you honestly when search deserves the first dollar. The full comparison is in search ads vs social ads for contractors.

How long does it take for Google Ads to work?

Clicks start the day it goes live, and inquiries usually follow within the first week. The account typically takes 30 to 60 days of search-term cleanup and bid changes to settle into a steady cost per appointment, so judge it on that window, not the first two weeks.

Do you run Google Ads for fence contractors?

Yes. Fence is our second trade after decks. Fence searches pull in a lot of people who will never hire an installer: repair jobs, DIY panels, temporary fence rental and installer job seekers. The negative keyword list goes in before launch, and the search terms get cleaned daily in the first weeks.

Who owns the Google Ads account?

You do. You own your ad accounts and your CRM data from day one, so you're never held hostage. If you ever leave, the account, its history and everything it learned stay with you.

Who is this not for?

Companies under about $500K a year, owners still doing the labor themselves, handymen and small repair work. Google Ads for emergency repair calls is a different business, and other agencies do it well.
