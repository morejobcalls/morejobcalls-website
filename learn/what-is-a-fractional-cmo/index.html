What Is a Fractional CMO? Role, Cost and When to Hire One

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com
Published November 3, 2026
Quick Answer

A fractional CMO is a part-time chief marketing officer: a senior head of marketing who works for your company a fraction of the week, under contract, instead of on payroll. They own the marketing plan, the budget and the results. Most charge a monthly retainer, commonly $3,000 to $15,000.

I run fractional CMO engagements for home-service companies. One of them, Eric E., a patio cover builder in Southern California, went from about $4M a year to about $8M a year in 12 months with us running his full marketing.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company that books exclusive sales appointments for high-ticket home-service contractors, and offers fractional CMO work for $2M+ companies. So I sell the thing this page explains, and I'll say where that matters.

Most companies already have a CMO. It's the owner, at 10pm, in the truck, reading an agency report on a phone with a cracked screen and trying to work out whether "impressions up 14%" is good news.

A fractional CMO takes that job off you. This page covers what the role actually is, what it costs, and the one question that separates a real one from a consultant with a nicer title. It's written for owners who run crews, because nearly every other article on this term was written for software startups.

Fractional CMO vs Full-Time CMO vs Marketing Agency

A fractional CMO sits between a full-time marketing executive and an agency. You get senior leadership and accountability for every channel, without a full salary. Here's how the three compare on what you actually care about.

For comparison, the median US marketing manager earned $166,790 in May 2025, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. That's a manager, not an executive, and it's before benefits, a laptop and the six months it takes to learn what a deck costs.

What Does a Fractional CMO Do?

A fractional CMO owns your whole marketing number, the way a full-time head of marketing would. They decide where the budget goes, oversee every channel and vendor, tie each closed job back to where it came from, and report it to you. In a home-service company, that list gets specific fast.

AreaWhat a fractional CMO owns
Plan and budgetWhere the money goes this quarter, what gets cut, what gets tested next
Paid advertisingSearch, Local Services Ads, social and retargeting, plus the creative
Website and trackingA site built to book, with every form and call tracked to its source
Local search and reviewsThe Google Business Profile, directory listings and a steady review flow
Follow-upHow fast leads get answered, reminders and no-show recovery
Past customersWin-backs and referrals from people who already hired you
ReportingA monthly report on signed jobs by channel, and a quarterly plan

What it doesn't own: selling, estimating, production, bookkeeping or hiring your crews. In the words of my own proposals: "You run the jobs. I fill the calendar at a pace you can handle and show where every dollar came from."

The word that matters in that table is "owns." An owner can delegate tasks to vendors all day long. What they can't delegate to a vendor is responsibility for the total, because every vendor is only responsible for its own slice (and every slice report somehow looks great).

Fractional CMO Cost and Hourly Rates

A fractional CMO usually costs $3,000 to $15,000 a month on retainer, or $150 to $350 an hour for hourly work, according to MarketerHire's 2026 pricing guide. Retainers usually buy 10 to 20 hours a week. Hourly work tends to run 5 to 15.

The retainer number is the least useful thing on this page, though. Two fractional CMOs can both charge $8,000 a month and be completely different purchases.

One gives you a plan and a monthly call. The other gives you a plan and a team that executes it. Same invoice, very different Tuesday.

The Question That Matters Most: Who Does the Work?

Chris W., deck builder, Gulf Shores, AL: $320,000 in jobs booked in about 2 months, then the ads paused while he hired. A marketing plan has to match what the crews can build.

Ask any fractional CMO this before you sign: when the plan says "launch the spring campaign," whose hands touch the ad account? There are two honest answers, and they're different products.

Strategy-only. They write the plan and manage your existing vendors. You still hire, pay and chase the people doing the work. That suits a company that already has good vendors and only lacks a leader.

Strategy plus execution. They write the plan and bring the team that runs it: ads, site, reviews and follow-up. That suits most home-service companies, because most of them don't have a bench of good vendors waiting for direction. They have three vendors who don't return each other's emails.

A fractional CMO who never touches the work is a consultant with a nicer title. That can still be worth paying for. Just know which one you're buying, because the slide deck looks the same either way.

When Does a Home-Service Company Need a Fractional CMO?

A home-service company usually needs a fractional CMO once it's past about $2M a year, pays several marketing vendors, and nobody inside the business owns the whole marketing number. Below that, the problem is usually an empty calendar, and more booked appointments fix it faster than a marketing leader would.

Common Questions

What does fractional CMO mean?

Fractional CMO means a part-time chief marketing officer: a senior head of marketing who works for your company a fraction of the week, under contract, instead of as a full-time employee. They own the marketing plan, the budget and the results, usually alongside a few other clients.

How much does a fractional CMO cost?

Most fractional CMOs charge a monthly retainer, which MarketerHire puts at $3,000 to $15,000 a month for most companies. That compares with an average base salary of $225,908 for a full-time US CMO, per Built In.

What the retainer covers matters more than the number: strategy only, or strategy plus the people who do the work.

What is a fractional CMO's hourly rate?

MarketerHire puts hourly fractional CMO work at $150 to $350 an hour, usually for 5 to 15 hours a week. Hourly billing suits a short project. For ongoing ownership of your marketing, a monthly retainer is more common.

Is a fractional CMO the same as a marketing agency?

No. An agency is usually hired to run one channel and is measured on that channel. A fractional CMO is accountable for all of your marketing, including the agencies. Some fractional CMOs only advise; others bring a team that also does the work.

When should a home-service company hire a fractional CMO?

Usually once the company is past about $2M a year, pays several marketing vendors, and nobody in the business owns the whole marketing number. Below that, more booked appointments usually fixes the problem faster than a marketing leader would.
