From 1 Crew to 5 in One Season: A Deck Builder's First Month

Spencer Wright — Founder, MoreJobCalls
July 4, 2026 · Updated September 2026
Key Takeaway

Jason B., a deck builder in Kennewick, Washington, went from one to three bids a week to two or three a day inside his first month. On roughly $1,600 of ad spend: more than $100,000 in signed deck jobs, around $60,000 of projected profit at his margins, and a calendar booked about six weeks out.

He started that season with one crew guy. He finished it with five. The part he keeps coming back to is not the volume, it is that he can now turn down work he does not want.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company for deck builders and other home-service contractors that books exclusive sales appointments onto the owner's calendar instead of selling shared leads. Jason is a client, and every number here comes from his own recorded check-in call.

The whole conversation, recorded about a month after launch:

Unscripted, from the call. Quotes below are his words.

Jason almost did not do this. He had been burned before, more than once: SEO companies, magazines, TV commercials. In his telling, thousands and thousands of dollars that never produced anything he could point at.

His wife was the hesitant one, which is the version of this story I hear most often. The money is not really the objection. The objection is the memory of the last three people who promised something similar.

His own summary of the pace: "We're adding another crew and another estimator and trying to keep up. It's been overwhelming in a good way."

The Change He Did Not Expect

Volume was not the part that surprised him. Recognition was.

"We recognize you from the Facebook stuff," homeowners were telling him at the door. Familiar face, before a word of sales. He put it this way: "They've already kind of looked into us and have some history with us without actually meeting any of them already. Seems like we're the first ones now versus the third or fourth."

That ordering is the whole game in a bidding trade. Arriving third or fourth means walking into a comparison someone else framed. Arriving first, to a homeowner who has already watched you explain your work, is a different conversation entirely: "That relationship's already started before we even get there."

It is the same mechanism covered in why contractor marketing got harder. Trust either gets built before the appointment or it gets negotiated during it, and the second version costs margin.

Booked Out Means Being Able to Say No

Six weeks of backlog changed how he chose work: "Not that I have to chase down every single customer and we can pick and choose them. The ones that you don't really want to work with, it's like, ah, we don't need that job."

Any contractor who has taken a job they regretted because the calendar looked thin understands the value of that sentence. It is also the point where marketing stops being a cost and starts functioning as leverage on which jobs you accept and what you charge for them.

His closing line on the whole month: "We haven't had this kind of lead flow since we started the business ever."

What Actually Produced This

Nothing exotic, and worth being plain about:

  1. His face in the ads, so homeowners met him on video before the driveway.
  2. Exclusive leads, not a list sold to four other deck builders in the same county.
  3. Qualification before booking, so the bids on the calendar were real projects.
  4. Enough consistency that recognition compounded across a season.

A fair caveat: his close rate sits around 20 to 30%, not the numbers you sometimes see quoted in this industry. The growth came from volume of qualified bids plus the ability to decline poor-fit work, not from some extraordinary conversion rate.

The Honest Summary

One month, about $1,600 of spend, six figures in signed deck work, and a crew that went from one to five across the season. The mechanism was not clever targeting. It was being recognized before he arrived, and being busy enough to choose.
