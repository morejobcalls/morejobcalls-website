Google Ads for Window Replacement: What Works and What Burns Money

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com
Published October 28, 2026
Quick Answer

Google Ads for window replacement works when your market has steady search volume, your keywords screen out repair shoppers, and someone answers fast. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average home improvement search lead at $90.92.

Judge it on cost per held appointment with every decision-maker present, not cost per click. Most window companies get more appointments pairing Google with Meta.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company that books exclusive sales appointments onto the calendars of high-ticket home-service contractors, window companies included. I've worked with 100+ home-service contractor businesses since 2024, and most of what we run is Meta. So my bias is about the size of a bay window, and I'll say plainly where Google beats what we sell.

Every November, a lot of homeowners tape plastic film over their windows, blow-dry it tight, and call that a heating plan. They've done it for six winters. They'll do it for a seventh. Not one of them typed "window replacement" into Google this week.

That's the whole puzzle of Google Ads for window replacement. Search ads are superb at catching the homeowner who finally gave up on the plastic. They do nothing for the ones still holding the hair dryer. This is the window-company deep dive from our guide to Google Ads for contractors, so it skips the basics.

Search vs Local Services Ads vs Performance Max for Window Companies

For most window companies, search ads should carry the account, Local Services Ads are worth it only with strong reviews and live phones, and Performance Max should wait until you have steady conversion volume to feed it. They charge differently and reach different homeowners.

Campaign typeYou pay forThe window-company catchVerdict
SearchA click"Window" searches attract repair, glass and software trafficThe core of the account, with tight keywords
Local Services AdsA leadGoogle's category is "window repair services," and homeowners can contact several companiesWorth testing with strong reviews and live phones
Performance MaxWhatever Google decidesLearns from form fills, and cheap form fills are rarely homeowners buying 14 windowsWait until you feed it real appointment data

Local Services Ads have a detail most window owners miss. Google's Local Services category list includes "Window repair services" and "Window cleaning services," and that's where the trade sits. You're a replacement company shopping in the repair aisle. It works for some companies, but expect calls about a stuck sash, and be quick to dispute the ones that don't fit.

Local Services Ads are also changing shape. WebFX reports they're "migrating into the Google Ads platform as a Performance Max for pay per lead goals campaign type," rolling out in phases from August 2026 through 2027. So what you set up this winter may look different by spring, which is roughly how window warranties feel to homeowners too.

Performance Max is the one I'd keep in the truck for now. It hunts for whatever conversion you tell it to count. Tell it a form fill counts and it'll find you the cheapest form fills on the internet, some of them from people who think "replacement window" means the little screen their dog pushed out. Letting it run free is like handing your teenager the company card and a list of suggestions.

Window Replacement Keywords That Bring Buyers, Not Repair Calls

The keywords that bring buyers name the full project: replacement, new windows, installation, a frame material, or a brand you sell, often with a town name. The ones that bring tire kickers name a single fix: repair, glass, a seal, a screen or a part. Bid on the first group and block the second.

The repair searcher isn't a bad person. He's a man with one foggy pane, a $300 budget, and a deep conviction that you can "just swap the glass." You paid for his click to learn that you don't, and he paid nothing to learn the same thing about you. That's the cheapest relationship he'll have all year.

Most PPC for window replacement goes wrong right here, in the match types. Use phrase and exact match on the buyer terms while the account is new. Broad match on the word "window" is an open invitation, and the internet always RSVPs.

Negative Keywords for Window Company PPC

Negative keywords stop your ads from showing on searches you don't want. For a window company, the five lists to build before launch are repair terms you don't serve, glass-only and auto glass, DIY and retail, other meanings of "windows," and job seekers. Google explains how they work in its negative keywords guide.

ListStarter negatives
Repair you don't dorepair, fix, crank, latch, balance, track, seal, screen, sash cord
Glass-only and autoglass only, pane, auto glass, windshield, car window, tint, film
DIY and retailDIY, how to, install yourself, Home Depot, Lowes, measure guide, parts
Other meaningsMicrosoft, Windows 11, laptop, update, cleaning, blinds, AC unit
Job seekers and freebiesjobs, hiring, salary, installer training, free windows, grant

The "other meanings" row is the one that hurts. Somewhere tonight, a man whose laptop won't stop updating will type "windows replacement help" and click your ad.

He doesn't want vinyl. He wants his fantasy football draft back. Block him before he costs you $8.33 and a little piece of your soul.

If you do sell window repair, don't negate it. Split it into its own campaign with its own budget, so a $300 job never gets custom-install prices. Then read the search terms report every day for the first few weeks, because the internet will invent searches you never imagined.

How to Screen Out One-Leggers Before Google Charges You Twice

A one-legger is an in-home appointment where only one decision-maker shows up. On Google Ads you pay for the click either way, so the screening has to happen on the landing page and form, and the account should be told which appointments were full-table so it bids toward more of them.

Ask on the landing page formWhat it screens out
Do you own the home?Renters asking about a landlord's windows
About how many windows?The single cracked pane
Who else will help decide?The one-legger, before it's booked
Which evening or Saturday works for both of you?The "they'll want to see this too" reschedule

Those questions cut the volume a little and the wasted evenings a lot. Then close the loop inside the ad account:

  1. Send Google the outcome. Google's offline conversion imports let you report which leads became held appointments or sales, so the bidding chases those instead of form fills.
  2. Count "held with every decision-maker" as your main conversion. It's the number that predicts the sale, so it's the number to optimize toward.

Sending the outcome is the step almost nobody wires up, and it's the one that matters most. An account that only sees form fills thinks a renter asking about a screen is a win. An account that sees held appointments learns, slowly and expensively, what a real window buyer looks like. I'd rather it learn that than learn your credit card number by heart.

Why Many Window Companies Pair or Replace Google With Meta

Window companies pair Google with Meta because Google only reaches homeowners already searching, while Meta reaches the much larger group living with old windows who haven't started looking. Meta leads also cost less: LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks put home improvement at $42.95 a lead, against $90.92 on search.

A cheaper lead isn't automatically a better one. A Facebook lead is earlier in the decision, so it needs faster follow-up and better screening. But windows are a want-it-eventually purchase, and the plastic-film households outnumber the searchers many times over. Meta is how you meet them before they've collected three quotes.

When Google Ads Should Be a Window Company's First Channel

Google Ads should come first for a window company in a big metro with steady search volume, with strong reviews and live phone coverage, or with a repair or glass division that wants search traffic anyway. Everywhere else, Google usually works best as the second channel, catching people who already know your name.

If this is youStart with

The second-channel role is underrated. Once your Meta ads have been in a neighborhood's feed for a month, some of those homeowners will search your company name. A small branded search campaign makes sure the competitor bidding on your name doesn't collect them at the door like a valet who doesn't work there.

Common Questions

Do Google Ads work for window replacement companies?

Yes, in markets with steady search volume and when someone answers the phone fast. Google Ads reach homeowners already shopping for new windows. They can't create more of those shoppers, so most window companies get more appointments by pairing Google with social ads.

How much does a window replacement lead cost on Google Ads?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average home and home improvement search lead at $90.92, with an $8.33 click. Window terms in a competitive metro often run higher. Judge the account on cost per held appointment and cost per sale, not cost per lead.

What negative keywords should a window company use?

Start with repair terms you don't serve, glass-only and auto glass terms, DIY and retail terms, other meanings of the word windows, and job-seeker terms. Then add new negatives from the search terms report every day for the first few weeks.

Should a window company use Local Services Ads?

Only with strong reviews and live phone coverage. Google's Local Services category for this trade is window repair services, so replacement companies should expect some repair-sized calls. The homeowner can also contact several companies from the same screen.

Is Google Ads or Facebook better for window companies?

Google catches homeowners already searching. Facebook and Instagram reach the much larger group with old windows who haven't started looking. Most window companies with crews to feed do best running social ads for volume and Google to catch searchers.

The Short Version

Google Ads for a window company comes down to four habits. Bid on the full project, block the repair aisle, screen for every decision-maker, and tell Google which appointments actually happened. If you'd rather not run that account yourself, our Google Ads management for contractors builds it inside your own account and judges it on cost per job.

And if your market's homeowners are still at the plastic-film stage, meet them in the feed first. The hair dryer has done enough. Let it retire.
