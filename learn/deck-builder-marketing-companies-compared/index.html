Deck Builder Marketing Companies Compared (2026)

Spencer Wright — Founder, MoreJobCalls
Updated September 2026
Key Takeaway

Deck builder marketing companies sell four different things: websites and SEO, ads managed for you, booked appointments, or shared lead records. Of the 15 companies below, three publish dollar prices on their own site, five publish a guarantee, and two state they take only one deck company per area.

Decide which of the four you're buying first. Then compare guarantees, pricing and exclusivity only between companies that sell the same thing.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company for deck builders and other home-service contractors that books exclusive sales appointments onto the owner's calendar instead of selling shared leads. MoreJobCalls.com is on this list and it's ours. It's listed alphabetically, not ranked, and I've described it with the same columns as everyone else.

I wrote this because most "top deck marketing agencies" lists are written by one of the agencies on them, ranked with themselves at number one, with nothing you can check. So this page doesn't rank anybody. Every cell for every other company came from that company's own website, and the sources are listed at the bottom so you can open the same pages I did. If you want a way to score them, that's a separate page: the nine-point scorecard for choosing a deck builder marketing agency.

How This Comparison Was Built

To make the list, a company needed a live page on its own site offering marketing to deck, outdoor living or home-service contractors. I opened each site in September 2026 and filled the same six columns for everyone: what they mainly sell, their deck or outdoor focus, any published guarantee, any published pricing model, and any exclusivity claim.

"None stated" and "not published" mean exactly that: I didn't find it on the pages listed in the sources. It doesn't mean the company won't offer it on a call. Quotes are theirs, word for word.

Which Type Fits You

Don't compare a $249 website plan to an appointment program and conclude one is overpriced. They're different products. Figure out which one you're shopping for, then compare inside that group.

If you are not sure which group you are shopping in yet, start with the five categories of lead generation company and come back to this table once you know.

You need a website and to show up on Google

If your site is old, your Google Business Profile is thin, or homeowners can't find you when they search your town plus "deck builder," start here. Footbridge Media, Builders of Authority, Contractor Gorilla, Slamdot, Socius Marketing, Superpath and Hook Agency all lead with websites and SEO. Deck Builder Marketers and Deck Builder Marketing do too, but only for deck companies. Footbridge publishes a flat monthly price and Hook publishes starting prices by service. The rest in this group quote on a call.

You want ads run in your own account

Nearly everyone on this list runs Google Ads, and many run Meta ads: Hardscape Marketing Crew, Hook Agency, Superpath, Fence & Deck Marketers, To The Max Media, Deck Builder Marketers and Deck Builder Marketing among them. The question that matters is who owns the ad account and the data if you leave. YardReach says its systems use "your accounts and your content." We build in the client's own accounts too. For everyone else, ask the question on the call and get the answer in writing. Socius says "anything we create is yours to own forever," which is the kind of line you want in the contract.

You want appointments on your calendar

If you have someone who can run three to five appointments a day and you'd rather not work inquiries yourself, look at the three that sell booked time: Dirt2Dollars (on-site appointments, strongest in land clearing and dirt work, with an outdoor living page), YardReach (consultations for pool, hardscape and outdoor living companies) and MoreJobCalls.com (decks first, plus other home-service trades). All three publish some form of guarantee, and they're worded very differently. Before you compare them, read what a contractor appointment guarantee really covers, especially what counts as an appointment and what voids the remedy.

You want a lead feed

Angi, Thumbtack, CraftJack, Houzz Pro and Google Local Services Ads aren't marketing companies in the sense of this table. They're marketplaces or platforms that sell access to homeowners, usually shared with other contractors, and you pay per lead or per month. They can make sense for a new company with no reviews. I compared them separately, on what a booked appointment ends up costing, in Angi alternatives for deck builders.

Two Kinds of "Exclusive"

This is the column people misread most. There are two separate promises hiding under the same word.

Territory exclusivity means the agency won't take on your competitor across town. Fence & Deck Marketers states one deck company per city. To The Max Media states one deck building and refinishing company per service area. We offer exclusive territory on our VIP tier only, not as a default.

Lead or appointment exclusivity means the homeowner they produce for you isn't sold to anyone else. Dirt2Dollars, YardReach and MoreJobCalls.com all state this. It matters for a deck company, because a shared homeowner turns a $40,000 project into a price fight. It's still not the same as keeping a competitor off the agency's roster, so ask which one you're being promised.

What I Left Off, and Why

I looked at several big home-service agencies, including Scorpion, Blue Corona (now merged with RYNO Strategic Solutions), Built Right Digital and 7ten Marketing. None of them had a deck or outdoor living page on their own site that I could find in September 2026, so they're not in the table. That's a statement about this table's scope, not about their work. If one of them adds a deck page, it belongs here and I'll add it.

How to Use This Table on a Sales Call

  • Ask for the all-in monthly number. Management fee plus ad spend, in writing.
  • Ask who owns the ad account, website and CRM, and what happens to each if you cancel.
  • Read the guarantee, not the headline. What counts, what the remedy is, what voids it.
  • Ask for deck results with a name attached. A builder you can call beats a percentage on a slide.

FAQ

Deck Builder Marketing Companies: Common Questions

What do deck builder marketing companies actually sell?

Four different things that all get called marketing. Most of the companies on this list sell search visibility: a website, SEO and Google Business Profile work, often with Google Ads on top. A smaller group runs paid ads, on Google, Meta or both, and hands you the inquiries. A few sell booked appointments, where the homeowner has already picked a time before you hear about them. And the marketplaces like Angi and Thumbtack sell shared lead records. Knowing which of the four you want narrows a list of fifteen down to three or four fast.

Which deck builder marketing companies publish their pricing?

As of September 2026, three of the fifteen publish dollar prices on their own site. Footbridge Media publishes a flat $249 a month. Hook Agency publishes starting prices by service. Hardscape Marketing Crew publishes three phase prices starting at $1,500 a month with ad spend separate. YardReach publishes its three pricing models, and Contractor Gorilla says its PPC management is a flat monthly fee, without dollar amounts. Everyone else, including MoreJobCalls.com, quotes on a call. Not publishing a price isn't a red flag by itself, but it does mean you should get the full number, fee plus ad spend, in writing before you sign.

Which deck builder marketing companies offer a guarantee?

Five put one on their site. Footbridge Media offers a 90-day money-back guarantee. Dirt2Dollars guarantees 30 on-site appointments in 90 days or keeps working at no charge until it does. Deck Builder Marketers has a Right Fit Guarantee that lets you walk away after three months. YardReach says it doesn't get paid unless you're happy with the results. MoreJobCalls.com guarantees 100 exclusive sales appointment opportunities in 100 days or less. Miss it, and you get your full management fee back, $2,000 from us, and free work until you hit 100. They cover very different things, so read what counts and what voids it before you compare them.

Which companies only work with one deck builder per market?

Two state it on their own sites. Fence & Deck Marketers says it works with one deck company and one fence company per city. To The Max Media says one deck building and refinishing company per service area while you're a client. MoreJobCalls.com offers exclusive territory on its VIP tier only. Don't confuse territory exclusivity with lead exclusivity. Dirt2Dollars, YardReach and MoreJobCalls.com all say the leads or appointments they produce go to one contractor, which is a different promise from keeping your competitor off the roster.

Is a deck-only marketing agency better than a multi-trade one?

Not automatically. A deck-only shop should know your season, your ticket size and what a homeowner needs to see before booking, which is a real advantage. A multi-trade shop may have more accounts to learn from. What decides it is whether they can show you deck results with named owners, who owns the ad account, and what happens between the click and the appointment. Score both kinds the same way with the scorecard in our guide to choosing a deck builder marketing agency.
