How to Pick a Facebook Ads Agency for Home Service Contractors

Spencer Wright — Founder, More Job Calls
August 28, 2026
Key Takeaway

Most agencies that show up when you search "Facebook ads agency for home service contractors" are generalists running the same playbook for a restaurant, an e-commerce store, and your deck company. Five things separate a specialist from a generalist: they only work with home service trades, your leads are exclusive to you, you own the ad account, the guarantee has a stated remedy in writing, and they measure booked appointments, not reach or engagement.

If an agency can't answer all five without hedging, you're looking at agency number seven, not the one that fixes it.

Quick disclosure: I run one of these agencies. My company builds Meta ad systems for home service contractors, deck builders first. I'm still going to give you the honest checklist, because the contractors I actually want as clients are the ones who vet this properly — not the ones who sign with whoever pitches the loudest.

Justin Wylie owns All Pro Decks & Patios in San Antonio. Before he found me, he'd hired six other agencies. Fifteen leads a month, around $400 each, all from Google, and by the time he called, the homeowner had already talked to three or four other contractors. He wasn't looking for someone with a better pitch. He'd heard every pitch. He was looking for someone who could actually be checked.

Why the Search Results Are Full of Generalists

Search "Facebook ads agency for home service contractors" and you'll mostly get agencies that list contractors as one vertical among ten — alongside dentists, restaurants, real estate agents, and e-commerce brands. That's not automatically disqualifying. But it means their process was built for the median client, and a home service sale doesn't work like the median client's sale.

A contractor's ideal buyer doesn't click an ad and check out in the same session. They see the ad, think about it for a few weeks while the project sits on their to-do list, and eventually reach out when the timing is right. An agency whose whole system is tuned for cost-per-click or "engagement" on a Tuesday will optimize for the wrong number, because the metric that matters — a booked appointment that shows up and buys — happens weeks after the click, and most reporting dashboards never connect the two.

The Five Things That Actually Matter

1. Do they only work with home service trades?

Not "have they worked with a contractor once." Ask how many active home service clients they run right now, and ask what they'd change about your ad account on day one. A specialist has an opinion in the first five minutes. A generalist asks you to fill out a discovery questionnaire.

2. Are your leads exclusive?

Some "Facebook ads agencies" are really shared-lead operations wearing an ads wrapper — the same homeowner's info gets sold or shown to multiple contractors. If a rep can't tell you plainly that every lead from your campaign goes to you and nobody else, assume it doesn't.

3. Who owns the ad account?

If the agency runs your ads from its own ad account and bills you monthly, you don't own the pixel data, the audience history, or the account itself. Leave that agency and you're starting from zero with the next one. Ask this question before you ask about pricing: whose name is on the ad account?

4. What's the guarantee, in writing?

Most "guaranteed leads" pitches resolve to one of three remedies: more free work, a refund of the fee (never the ad spend, which went straight from your card to the platform), or a cash payment. Only the third one actually costs the agency something if they miss. Ask what they owe you if they don't deliver, and ask to see the clause — not the sales page, the contract.

5. What do they report — bookings, or vanity metrics?

Reach, impressions, and "engagement" don't pay your crews. Ask what number they check every Monday morning. If the honest answer isn't "booked appointments" or "cost per booked job," you're paying for a marketing report, not a pipeline.

What It Looks Like When It's Done Right

Justin's numbers, fourteen days after switching: fifteen leads a month at roughly $400 each became thirty a week at under $30 each, and in the first week alone he put out almost a million dollars in quotes. He's the longest-running client on the system now — six agencies, then one that stuck.

Chip Paynter is a 33-year veteran general contractor in California who'd sent out only five or six deck bids in the first ten months of 2025, running mostly on referrals. Within 21 days of launching a Meta ad system built for his trade, he had over 60 high-intent leads and converted more than $200,000 in booked work — four projects between $40,000 and $65,000. He described the leads as different: not the marketplace beauty contest where five contractors fight over one price-shopper, homeowners who already felt like they knew him before he showed up.

Billy Stewart had spent $250,000 on leads over his career through the usual channels before switching. He closed $300,000 in work in 17 days on $1,657 of ad spend.

None of these are Facebook ad "hacks." They're what happens when the agency running the account has done nothing but home service trades long enough to know what an appointment is actually worth, and has a reason — a real one, on the line — to protect that number instead of a vanity metric.

The Honest Verdict

A generalist agency isn't running a scam. They're usually competent at Facebook ads in general. The problem is that "competent at Facebook ads in general" and "knows how a $45,000 deck sale actually happens" are two different skill sets, and only one of them is what you're paying for. Run every agency you're considering through the five questions above, including mine. Whoever can't answer without hedging just told you what you need to know.
