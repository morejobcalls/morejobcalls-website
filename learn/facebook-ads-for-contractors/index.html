Facebook Ads for Contractors: What Actually Books Jobs in 2026

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com
Published November 11, 2026
Quick Answer

Facebook ads for contractors put the owner and real finished jobs in front of homeowners who want a project but aren't shopping for a contractor yet. Home improvement lead campaigns averaged $42.95 a lead in LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks. Judge them on cost per completed appointment and signed job, not cost per lead.

They fit planned projects like kitchens, additions, roofs and decks far better than emergencies. I've worked with 100+ home-service contractor businesses since 2024, and most accounts that fail, fail after the click.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company that books exclusive sales appointments for high-ticket home-service contractors, mostly with Facebook and Instagram ads. So I have a stake in this answer, and I'll say so where it matters.

Somewhere right now a general contractor is boosting a photo of a backsplash for $20. It'll reach 1,400 strangers, collect nine likes and earn one comment from his cousin. Two weeks later he'll tell a guy at the supply house that Facebook doesn't work.

He's half right. Boosted photos don't work. The real version works well for planned projects, and it has very little to do with likes.

Pew's 2025 survey found 71% of U.S. adults use Facebook, and 80% of those aged 30 to 49, so your homeowners are in there. The question is what they're doing when your ad shows up.

Do Facebook Ads Work for Contractors?

Yes, for planned projects, and much less for emergencies. A homeowner who wants a new kitchen has wanted it since the dishwasher started making that noise. They aren't searching yet, so an ad in their feed is the first contractor they've heard from all year.

Nobody with water coming through the ceiling is scrolling for inspiration. That homeowner is on Google, on hold, holding a bucket, and your Facebook ad can't help them.

What Do Facebook Ads Cost a Contractor?

Home improvement lead campaigns average $42.95 a lead and $2.18 a click, according to LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks. That's the sticker price. It's about as useful for planning your year as the price of a single 2x4 is for bidding an addition.

The number that decides whether you made money is cost per completed appointment. It takes your ad spend and divides it by homeowners who actually sat down with you. Then multiply by how many appointments it takes you to sign one job, and you've got your real ad cost per job.

Here's that math on one account, and yes, it's a deck builder, because it's the cleanest math I can show you. Chris W., custom deck and home builder in Gulf Shores, AL, spent $5,109.15 on ads over about 60 days.

How Facebook Ads for Contractors Work, From Click to Contract

The ad is one step of six, and it's the one that gets all the attention. The other five are where most of the money leaks out.

  1. The ad stops a homeowner mid-scroll. Usually the owner on camera, talking about one kind of job.
  2. A page explains what happens next. Who you are, what you build, and what the homeowner gets by booking.
  3. A few questions sort the good fits. Project type, location and timeline, so you aren't driving 50 miles to price a screen door.
  4. The homeowner picks a time on your calendar. A date they chose themselves is one they're far more likely to keep.
  5. Calls, texts and reminders hold the appointment. A real person calling quickly matters most here.
  6. You make the offer in the home. Price it at the kitchen table and ask for the job while both decision-makers are sitting there.

That last step isn't an ad setting, and it might be the most expensive one on the list. Emailing the number from your truck two days later gives the homeowner plenty of quiet time to get two more bids and a suggestion from their brother-in-law.

What a Contractor's Facebook Ad Needs to Show

It needs the owner, real jobs and one clear next step. Homeowners are letting a stranger into their house for a big check, so the ad's first job is to make you stop being a stranger.

"Quality craftsmanship and great customer service" has been on contractor ads since the invention of the van. Nobody has ever read it and thought, thank goodness, one who doesn't do bad work on purpose. Say the thing only you can say: the job, the town, the number of years, the customer's own sentence.

And if you hate the idea of being on camera, welcome. Every owner hates it. Then they watch the first clip back, ask for a do-over because of their hat, and find out homeowners don't care about the hat.

Facebook Ads for General Contractors: Sell One Job, Not the Whole License

General contractors should run each ad about one job they want more of, not a list of everything they can build. A homeowner dreaming about a primary bath addition doesn't want a company that does "additions, decks, siding, roofing, drywall and light commercial." That reads like the side of a van driven by a man who has never once said no.

This is the mistake that's special to GCs and remodelers, because you really can do all of it. Do all of it after the contract. Before the contract, the ad gets to be about one thing.

Instead of this adRun these

It works for whole-home work, too. One remodeling company had about $1 million in new bids out within 30 days of launch, from dozens of leads. Those are bids, not closed jobs, but that's a very different month from waiting on the next referral.

Instant Form or Landing Page?

I prefer a page with a few qualifying questions and a calendar. Instant forms, the ones that fill in inside Facebook, usually cost less per lead. They're also so easy that a homeowner can respond with one thumb during a commercial, then not remember doing it.

Facebook Ads vs Google Ads for Contractors

Facebook reaches homeowners before they're shopping, and Google reaches them while they are. LocaliQ's 2026 search benchmarks put a home improvement lead on Google at $90.92 and a click at $8.33, against $42.95 and $2.18 on Facebook. Google's lead is often closer to buying, which is what you're paying extra for.

The catch is that the Google homeowner opened four tabs, so you're in a price race by lunchtime. I lean Facebook for planned projects, and yes, it's the thing I sell, so read the full comparison of Google Ads and Meta ads and argue with it. If search fits your trade, start with Google Ads for contractors.

Why Most Contractor Facebook Ads Fail After the Click

Most of them fail on the phone, not in the ad account. Homeowners respond, then nobody reaches them fast enough. Invoca's 2026 report found only 52% of callers to home-services businesses speak with a person, and that's the ones calling you.

Timing matters as much as answering. A Harvard Business Review study found firms that tried to reach a lead within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited even one hour longer. That study is old, and homeowners haven't gotten more patient since.

  1. Someone calls within minutes during the day. A foreman on a ladder doesn't count, however good his intentions.
  2. The homeowner gets a text the moment they respond. It proves a real company got the message.
  3. Every appointment gets reminders. You still drove there if they forgot.
  4. Someone tracks what happened to each lead. Otherwise you'll judge the ads by how the last two calls felt.

Should a Contractor Run Facebook Ads or Hire Someone?

Run them yourself if you have a few focused hours every week and someone reliable answering the phone. Hire it out if your evenings already belong to estimates. You'll check Ads Manager the way you check a job you know is fine, at 9 p.m., from the driveway, in socks, and it'll change nothing.

If you hire, make sure you own your ad accounts and your CRM data from day one. Then use this guide to picking a Facebook ads agency to ask the right questions. The agency that gets annoyed by them just answered the most important one.

Common Questions

Do Facebook ads work for contractors?

Yes, for planned projects like kitchens, additions, decks, roofs and siding. Those homeowners have wanted the job for months and aren't shopping yet. They work much less well for emergencies, where the homeowner goes straight to Google.

How much do Facebook ads cost for a contractor?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put home improvement lead campaigns at $42.95 a lead and $2.18 a click. On one deck account we run, a completed appointment came to about $122 and a signed job to about $426 in ad spend. Your numbers depend on your market and follow-up.

How much should a contractor budget for Facebook ads?

There's no honest one-size number. Work backward from what you can spend to sign one job and how many appointments your crews can run. We set budgets on a strategy call, after seeing the market and the capacity.

Are Facebook ads better than Google Ads for contractors?

For planned projects, usually yes, because you reach the homeowner before they're collecting bids. For emergencies, Google wins, because that homeowner is searching right now. Many contractors end up running both.

Should a contractor use a Facebook lead form or a landing page?

I prefer a page with a few qualifying questions and a calendar. Instant forms make it so easy to respond that many people forget they did. A little effort up front means fewer dead numbers.

Where to Start

Pick the job you want more of, film the owner talking about it, and send the ad to a page with your calendar on it. Then judge it on completed appointments and signed jobs.
