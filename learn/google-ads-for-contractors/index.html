The Google Ads Campaign Types, in Contractor Terms

Google Ads is Google's paid advertising system. For contractors it mainly means search ads at the top of the results, Local Services Ads above those, and a handful of automated campaign types that spend your money in places you'll never see.

Campaign typeWhat it isHow you payUse it when
SearchText ads on the results page for the searches you choosePer clickAlmost always. It's the core of a contractor account
Local Services AdsThe "Google Guaranteed" style listings at the very topPer leadYou have strong reviews and answer the phone live
Performance MaxGoogle decides where your ads run, across all its propertiesPer click or viewYou already produce plenty of conversions every month
Display and YouTubeBanner and video ads on other sites and videosPer view or clickRetargeting people who already visited your site

Performance Max deserves a warning label. It learns from conversions, and a contracting company producing a handful of leads a week doesn't feed it enough to learn. What you get instead is your budget wandering around YouTube like a kid let loose in a hardware store.

Do Google Ads Work for Contractors?

Google Ads work for contractors whose customers search with urgency, in markets big enough to have steady search volume. They work less well for planned projects in small markets, because search can only capture demand that already exists. It can't create more.

Google usually fits firstGoogle usually fits second
TradesHVAC replacement, storm roofing, emergency repairDecks, fences, patio covers, pergolas, siding, windows
WhySomething broke, so the homeowner searches todayNothing broke, so they think about it for months first
Market sizeBig metros with steady searchesTowns where a few dozen people search a month
What wins the clickShowing up first and answering fastBeing trusted before they compare quotes

A furnace that dies in January sends the homeowner to Google before they've found a sweater. A deck sends nobody anywhere. The family talks about it every summer, buys patio furniture "for when we get the deck," and keeps talking.

The small-market version of this is where owners get burned. Josiah W., a deck builder in Montana's Flathead Valley, was told flat out not to run ads at all in a market of about 90,000 people. He ended up getting 40 to 50 leads a month anyway.

How to Set Up Google Ads for a Contracting Business

Set up conversion tracking first, then a tight service area, one campaign per service, a negative keyword list, and a landing page for each service. Build it all paused, review it, and only then turn it on.

  1. Track calls and forms before anything spends. Otherwise you'll know what it cost and never what it produced.
  2. Draw the service area you actually drive to. Not the whole state because your cousin once did a job there.
  3. Split campaigns by service. Someone searching "composite deck builder" should see a composite deck ad, not "Quality Home Improvements Since 1998."
  4. Load the negative keyword list before launch. More on that just below.
  5. Send each service to its own page. Your homepage is a lobby. A landing page is the sales desk.
  6. Add call and location details to the ads. One tap to call beats a form on a phone.
  7. Read the search terms report daily for the first weeks. It shows the searches you actually paid for, which are often a surprise.

Negative keywords are the searches you tell Google to never show you for. Every trade has its own junk. These are the usual starting points.

TradeTypical negative keywords to start with
DecksDIY, plans, kits, calculator, Home Depot, Lowes, stain
Fencerepair, panels, temporary, rental, post hole digger, jobs
Roofingleak repair (if you only replace), shingles for sale, jobs, salary

Every tradefree, cheap, how to, YouTube, school, license, careers

Treat these as a starting list, then add to it every day from your own search terms. The account that skips this step funds a lot of DIY weekends.

Local Services Ads vs Search Ads for Contractors

Local Services Ads charge per lead and sit at the very top of the page. Search ads charge per click and sit below them. In a 2026 WebFX test of 500 home-services searches, Local Services Ads showed on 28.4% of them, and always in first position.

What showed upShare of 500 home-services searches
Map listings (local pack)97.6%
Local Services Ads, searches with a city name49%
Local Services Ads, all searches28.4%
Regular search ads8.4%

Source: WebFX, 2026. One agency's snapshot from May 2026, across HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical and garage doors.

That map-listing number is the quiet headline: your Google Business Profile shows up far more often than any ad. It's free, and local SEO for contractors covers how to make it win. Local Services Ads come with a catch, too. The homeowner can contact several companies from the same screen, so the one who calls back first usually gets the job.

The Mistakes That Burn Contractor Budgets

Most contractor Google Ads accounts don't fail on budget. They fail on setup and follow-up: no tracking, no negative keywords, one campaign for every service, and leads that wait until tomorrow for a callback.

  • Missed calls. Invoca's 2026 home-services report found only 52% of callers reach a person. You paid for the other 48% too.
  • Slow callbacks on forms. In the lead response study run with MIT, calling at 30 minutes instead of 5 made contact 100 times less likely. It's from 2007, and homeowners haven't gotten more patient since.
  • Reporting clicks instead of jobs. Clicks always go up. That's what makes them such a comfortable thing to report.
  • Emailing the quote. If the homeowner sat with you and left without hearing a price, the presentation never happened. Give the number at the table.
  • Letting someone else own the account. Keep the ad account in your name, so the history stays with you if the agency doesn't.

When Google Ads Is the Wrong First Channel

Google Ads is the wrong first channel when you sell planned projects in a small market, when nobody can answer the phone during the day, or when you need more appointments than your area is currently searching for. In those cases, reach homeowners before they search, and add Google second.

Common Questions

Do Google Ads work for contractors?

Yes, when enough people in your service area search for what you sell and someone answers the phone fast. Google Ads reach homeowners who are already looking.

They can't create demand, so in a small market or a planned-project trade they run out of searches quickly.

How much do Google Ads cost for contractors?

LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put the average home and home improvement search click at $8.33 and the average lead at $90.92. Your cost per signed job depends on how many leads book, show up and sign, so work the whole chain, not just the click.

What type of Google Ads campaign is best for contractors?

For most contractors, a narrow Search campaign with a strong negative keyword list, plus Local Services Ads if you have good reviews. Performance Max usually needs more conversions than a contractor produces to learn well.

How long do Google Ads take to work for contractors?

Clicks start the day ads go live, and inquiries usually follow within the first week. The account typically takes 30 to 60 days of search-term cleanup to settle into a steady cost per appointment.

Should contractors run Google Ads or Facebook ads?

Google catches people already searching. Facebook and Instagram reach homeowners before they search.

Urgent trades usually start with Google. Planned projects like decks, fences and patios usually get more appointments from social ads, with Google as the second channel.
