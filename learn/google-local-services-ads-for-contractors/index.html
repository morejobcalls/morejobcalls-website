Google Local Services Ads for Contractors: How They Work in 2026

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com

They work best for contractors with strong reviews and a phone that gets answered live, because the homeowner can contact several companies from one screen. I've worked with 100+ home-service contractor businesses since 2024, and that phone is where most of these ads are won or lost.

What Are Google Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads are Google's pay-per-lead ads for local service businesses. They show your business name, star rating, review count and a verification badge, and the homeowner can call or message you straight from the ad. You don't pay for the click. You pay when a homeowner contacts you about a job you do.

What the homeowner seesWhat it means for you

Other companies on the same screenThe homeowner can contact several of you at once

Two things jump out. The map shows up on almost everything, so your Business Profile and reviews matter more than any ad. And Local Services Ads showed most on city searches, about 49% of them, against about 25% of other searches.

How You're Charged for Local Services Ads

You pay per lead, and Google spells out the rules on its own "How leads work" help page. The short version is better than pay-per-click, with a catch in the price.

The ruleIn Google's words
What you pay for"Valid leads are related to your business"
What sets the price"location, the job type, the type of lead, or your bidding mode"
Junk at first contact"leads determined to be invalid or low quality are not charged"
Junk found laterCharged leads are reassessed over 30 days, and "a credit is automatically applied"

That credit system is genuinely useful. Still, check your lead list every week and flag anything that wasn't a real job through the lead's feedback survey. Nobody at Google is going to call you about the guy asking whether you install hot tubs.

Who Local Services Ads Work For

Local Services Ads lean toward urgent, local searches. In the WebFX study they showed on 21% of emergency searches and 34% of near-me searches, but also on 25% of high-ticket ones. That makes them strongest for trades where the homeowner needs someone soon: HVAC service, roof repair, garage doors, plumbing.

If you sell big planned projects, like a deck, a reside or a full HVAC replacement, they're one useful channel, not the whole plan. The homeowner planning a project months out often isn't searching yet. That's the buyer our appointment system is built to reach, and yes, that's my stake.

How to Win the Lead Once You Pay for It

Here's the catch with Local Services Ads. You paid for the lead, but the homeowner may have tapped two other companies too. The fastest real answer usually wins, and most businesses aren't fast.

What happensNumberSource
Callers to home-services businesses who reach a person52%Invoca, 2026
Drop in contact odds calling at 30 minutes instead of 5100 times lowerInsideSales and MIT, 2007
Consumers who won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews47%BrightLocal, 2026
  1. Answer live during business hours. A Local Services lead that hits voicemail is money you paid to send a homeowner to the next company.
  2. Reply to message leads in minutes. Message leads count too, and they go cold just as fast.
  3. Keep the reviews coming. Your rating is the ad. A few new reviews every month beats a big push once a year.
  4. Track signed jobs, not leads. Log which leads became jobs, so you know what each one actually earned you.

What's Changing in 2026

Local Services Ads are moving. According to the editor's note on the WebFX study, they're "rolling out in phases from August 2026 through 2027" as a Performance Max campaign type inside Google Ads. Expect the setup screens to change before the basics do.

The basics should hold: pay per lead, reviews up front, the phone deciding who wins. If you run them yourself, check your account settings after each change rolls out. Defaults have a way of quietly widening your service area.

What We See in the Field

Across the 100+ contractor businesses I've worked with, the patterns with Local Services Ads are consistent.

  1. The phone decides it. Two contractors in the same town can get very different results from the same ads, and it's almost always the answer rate.
  2. Reviews are the price of entry. A thin profile at the top of the page just shows homeowners who to skip.
  3. They're a channel, not a plan. They catch homeowners who are ready now, and they do little for the one who's still planning.

Common Questions

Are Google Local Services Ads worth it for contractors?

They're worth it when you have solid reviews and someone answers the phone live during the day. They sit at the top of the page and you pay per lead, but the homeowner can contact several companies from the same screen, so the fastest answer usually wins.

How much do Local Services Ads cost per lead?

It varies. Google says lead prices depend on your location, the job type, the type of lead and your bidding mode. The number to watch is cost per signed job.

Can I get a refund for a bad Local Services lead?

Google says invalid or low-quality leads aren't charged, and charged leads are reassessed over 30 days, with a credit applied automatically if a lead turns out to be poor quality, a duplicate or invalid. You can also flag a lead through its feedback survey.

What's the difference between Local Services Ads and Google Ads?

Local Services Ads charge per lead and show your reviews at the very top of the page. Regular Google search ads charge per click and send the homeowner to your website.

Do Local Services Ads work for high-ticket contractors?

Partly. They show up most on urgent, local searches, so a contractor selling big planned projects usually needs another channel alongside them.
