Why Fence Is a Seasonal, Quote-Shopped Job

Fence is a mid-ticket job that homeowners shop hard and mostly buy in spring. The average homeowner spends between $2,000 and $5,000 on a new fence, and spring is the busiest install season, per NerdWallet's fence cost guide. Both facts change how you should advertise.

Start with the ticket. A fence costs about as much as a used jet ski, which is exactly the price where a homeowner gets three quotes "just to be safe." Nobody gets three quotes on a $40 pizza. Nobody skips them on a $4,000 fence. So you're rarely the only number on the kitchen counter, and the first solid one has a real head start.

Then the season. The dog escapes in March. The homeowner spends two weekends propping a pallet against the gap like it's a medieval siege.

By April they're calling everyone. If your ads only switch on when your crews are free, you show up after the decision. I'd rather be in the feed in February, when that pallet is still holding.

Fence Company Google Ads and Local Services Ads

Fence company Google Ads work for the homeowner who's already typing "fence installers near me," and they cost real money per click. LocaliQ's 2026 search advertising benchmarks put the home and home improvement category at $8.33 per click and $90.92 per lead. That's a lead, not a booked estimate.

Search is the right channel for the buyer who already decided. It's also the channel every competitor in your county is bidding on, including the guy whose truck says "Since 1987" in a font that's also from 1987. Run it narrow. Bid on install terms like "fence company" and "vinyl fence installation." Add negatives for "DIY," "panels," "Home Depot" and "how to." Those searchers will spend your budget learning to do it themselves.

Local Services Ads are the boxes at the very top with the Google badge. Google says you pay only for leads from those ads, not clicks. The catch: the homeowner sees several companies on the same screen and can message three of them before lunch. Reviews and an answered phone decide who wins there.

Look at the last bar. A lead and a booked appointment are different animals, and the gap between them is where most ad budgets quietly disappear. Keep that in mind for every channel below.

Facebook Ads for Fence Companies

Facebook ads for fence companies reach the homeowner who needs a fence and hasn't searched for one yet, which is most of them. LocaliQ's 2026 Facebook benchmarks put home and home improvement lead campaigns at $42.95 per lead, less than half the search figure above. This is the channel we run most, inside the client's own ad account.

Think about the pallet guy. He isn't googling yet. He's scrolling at 9pm, half-watching a show, while the dog stares at the pallet like it owes him money. An ad with a real fence from his side of town, and the owner who built it talking for 20 seconds, catches him a month before he'd ever search.

What the ad needs is simple: the owner on camera, real local work and one clear offer.

We write the script, the owner records it on their phone, and we edit and test it. Then the ad sends people to a short application that asks about material, footage, timeline and budget, so the calendar only fills with people who are ready.

Nextdoor, Yard Signs and Direct Mail for Fence Companies

Neighborhood channels work unusually well for fence, because every finished job is visible from the street.

Nextdoor says 1 in 3 U.S. households are active on the platform. USPS Every Door Direct Mail costs about 26 cents per piece in postage. And a yard sign costs less than lunch.

The yard sign is the most underrated ad in the trade. A new fence is a billboard that neighbors walk past twice a day with a dog on a leash.

Some of those dogs are plotting. Their owners know it. A sign with your name and number turns that walk into a quote request.

Nextdoor and mail do the same job on purpose. Pick the streets around a finished install and tell them who built the fence they keep walking past. Do the math before you print, though:

Neighborhood channelWhat it costsWhere it earns its keep
Yard signA few dollars per signEvery install, left up as long as the homeowner allows
Direct mail, 2,000 homesAbout $520 in postage at 26 cents a piece (USPS), plus printingThe routes around a job you just finished
Nextdoor adsYou set the budgetNeighborhoods where people already talk about your work

None of these will fill a calendar on their own. They're the cheap layer that makes your paid ads convert better, because the homeowner has seen your name on the corner twice before your ad shows up.

Shared-Lead Marketplaces for Fence Company Leads

Shared-lead marketplaces sell the same homeowner to several fence companies, so you're buying a spot in a price race. On a $4,000 fence with five bidders, the cheapest number usually takes it. They can plug a gap in a slow week. They're a poor foundation for a fence business.

Here's how it feels from your side. The phone dings, and you call in four minutes, feeling fast and proud.

The homeowner says "oh, a few of you have called already" in the tone of a person who's been called by a few of you already. You knock $300 off before you've seen the yard. Congratulations, you've won the job and lost the margin.

Common Questions

What's the best way to advertise a fence company?

Run two layers. Google search and Local Services Ads catch homeowners who are already shopping for a fence. Facebook and Instagram ads reach the much bigger group who need one and haven't started looking. Back both with yard signs and a fast first quote.

How much should a fence company spend on advertising?

Work backwards from estimates, not from a percentage. Decide how many estimates your crews can quote and install each week, then find the ad spend that books that many. Hold it steady for 60 days and judge it on cost per booked estimate.

Do Google Ads work for fence companies?

Yes, for the homeowner who's already searching. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put home and home improvement search ads at $8.33 a click and $90.92 a lead. Keep the keywords tight to installation terms, add DIY and supply-store negatives, and send clicks to a page about fences, not your homepage.

Are Facebook ads good for fence companies?

Yes, because most homeowners who need a fence aren't searching for one yet. LocaliQ's 2026 benchmarks put home and home improvement lead campaigns on Facebook at $42.95 a lead. The ad works best with the owner on camera, real local fences and one clear offer, followed by a fast call.

When should a fence company advertise?

Before spring, not during it. Spring is the busiest season for fence installs, so the homeowners who sign in April are deciding in February and March. Run ads through late winter and sell a spring install slot.
