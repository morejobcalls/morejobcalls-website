Contractor Advertising: 9 Options Ranked by Cost Per Booked Appointment

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com

Quick Answer

The best contractor advertising is whatever books a homeowner's appointment for the least money, not whatever sells the cheapest lead. After referrals, signs and reviews, Facebook and Instagram ads and Google Local Services Ads cost about $107 and $121 per booked appointment on published numbers. Google search runs about $227.

Those figures are my math, with the sources and assumptions shown below. I've worked with 100+ home-service contractor businesses since 2024, and across them the spread is wide enough that your own tracking beats any benchmark.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company that books exclusive sales appointments for high-ticket home-service contractors, mostly with Facebook and Instagram ads. So I sell one of the channels ranked here, and I'll say so where it matters.

Ask ten contractors how they advertise and nine will say word of mouth, usually while standing next to a truck with no phone number on it. The tenth is paying for all of it at once, and he has a binder.

Page one of Google isn't much help. It's mostly lists of marketing ideas, and "try TikTok" sits next to "sponsor a Little League team" with no hint of which one pays. So here are the options ranked by the only number that matters before the sale: what it costs to get a homeowner on your calendar.

The Cheap Contractor Advertising You Should Already Have

Referrals, signs and your Google Business Profile come first because they cost almost nothing and make every paid channel work better. A homeowner who sees your ad will look you up. If they find a two-star rating and a profile photo of a tailgate, you just paid to send them to somebody else.

  1. Ask for referrals on purpose. Ask at the walkthrough, when the homeowner is standing in the new thing and feeling good about writing you that last check.
  2. Put a sign in every yard you're allowed to. Neighbors spend the whole job watching your crew through the blinds, so give them a phone number while they're at it.
  3. Letter the truck. It's the only ad you'll ever buy that also carries lumber.
  4. Work your reviews. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. The steps are in local SEO for contractors.

The trouble with all four is the same. You can't turn them up in a slow month, and referrals tend to dry up at exactly the moment you've got two crews and nothing on the board.

Is Direct Mail Worth It for Contractors?

It can be, mostly around a job you just finished, but you have to track it yourself. USPS Every Door Direct Mail runs 26 cents a piece for postage, with a 200-piece minimum and a 5,000-piece daily maximum per ZIP Code. So 5,000 postcards is $1,300 in postage before anyone's printed anything.

The 2024 ANA report, as cited by PI World, puts prospect-list response at 4% to 5%. That's all industries, and a response isn't an appointment. Your mother-in-law also opens everything, and she isn't getting a new roof.

Give every mailer its own phone number or web link, then count what it books. If you can't count it, it isn't ranked seventh, it's unranked.

Are Shared Lead Marketplaces Worth It?

They're usually the most expensive way to get a conversation, even when the lead looks cheap. Home Service Direct lists Angi and HomeAdvisor deck leads at $75 to $175, each shared with 3 to 5 other builders. The price looks fine. Then you call.

At 2 in 10, a $75 lead is really a $375 conversation, and that's before they've agreed to anything. By then the homeowner has heard from four of you and has started letting the phone ring, with the expression of someone being followed around a car lot. Construction Company Advertising: Know Who's Writing the Check

Construction company advertising splits by who's paying. Homeowners respond to ads and reviews. Developers, property managers and commercial owners mostly respond to relationships and past work.

Your customerWhat reaches themWhat doesn't
HomeownersFacebook, Google, reviews, neighbors, job signsYour bonding capacity
Property managersDirect outreach, referrals from other managers, a fast response on small jobsA Facebook ad about their dream kitchen
Developers and commercial ownersRelationships, past projects, a website that proves itYard signs, though they do notice them

Most construction company advertising ideas you'll find assume you sell to homeowners. If you don't, skip the ad platforms for now and spend that money on lunch with property managers. I don't have a benchmark for that one, but the sandwiches beat anything in an ad account.

Construction company advertising ideas worth trying

  1. A one-minute video of each finished job, with the owner walking through it.
  2. A letter to the 20 nearest neighbors before you start, so the noise comes with your name on it.
  3. A project page for every big job, so your website proves you built it.
  4. A printed card for your subs and suppliers. They hear about work before you do.

General Contractor Advertising: Pick the Work You Want

General contractors should advertise the jobs they want more of, not everything they're able to do. Your skill is range, but range makes a terrible ad. A homeowner who wants a basement finished is looking for a basement person, even if you've also built two churches and a bowling alley.

List your last 20 jobs by profit, not revenue, then advertise the top two kinds. It takes one evening, and the answer often isn't the work you've been telling people you do. Our general contractor marketing is built around that same choice.

Remodeling Advertising Ideas That Book Appointments

Remodeling sells on trust and pictures, so the best ideas show your actual work and your actual face. A homeowner is about to let you take a wall out of their house, so a clip-art hammer isn't closing that gap.

Common Questions

What is the best advertising for contractors?

Referrals and signs are cheapest, but you can't buy more of them when the calendar is empty. Among channels you can scale, Facebook and Instagram ads and Google Local Services Ads come out lowest per booked appointment on published numbers, at about $107 and $121.

How much does contractor advertising cost?

It depends on the channel. Published 2026 averages are about $43 a lead on Facebook, $53 on Local Services Ads and $91 on Google search for home improvement. Compare them after working out what each costs per booked appointment.

How do I advertise my construction company?

Start with the cheap things you own: signs, your Google Business Profile and reviews. Then add one paid channel that fits how your customers buy. Track what each one costs per appointment before adding another.

How do general contractors get more clients?

Most run on referrals until those run out. Pick the jobs you want more of, advertise those specifically, show real finished work, and have someone answer every inquiry quickly.

Is direct mail worth it for contractors?

It can be, especially around a job you just finished. Postage alone is 26 cents a piece for USPS Every Door Direct Mail, so give every mailer its own phone number or link. Otherwise you can't tell what it booked.

Where to Start

Get the cheap things right first: signs, reviews and referrals. Then pick one paid channel that matches how your customers buy, and judge it on cost per booked appointment.
