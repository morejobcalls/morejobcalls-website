Local SEO for Contractors: How to Win the Map in 2026

Spencer Wright · Founder, MoreJobCalls.com
Published October 22, 2026
Quick Answer

Local SEO for contractors is how you show up when homeowners near you search for your service: in the Google map results, the regular results under them, and now AI answers. Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and popularity.

For a contractor, that comes down to three things you control: a complete Google Business Profile, a steady flow of reviews, and a website with a real page for each service you sell.

I've worked with 100+ home-service contractor businesses since 2024, from Montana towns of 90,000 people to big metros. The map plays by different rules in each, and this guide covers both.

MoreJobCalls.com is a marketing company that books exclusive sales appointments onto the calendars of high-ticket home-service contractors, and builds SEO for them standalone or alongside it. So I have a stake in this answer, and I'll say so where it matters.

Search "deck builder near me" in most towns and you'll find at least one listing called something like "Bob's Decks Best Deck Builder Near Me Cheap Affordable." Bob is trying very hard. Bob is also breaking Google's rules, and we'll come back to Bob.

Local SEO has a reputation for being mysterious, mostly because the people selling it like it that way. Google publishes how the map works. The hard part is doing the boring parts every week for a year.

What Is Local SEO for Contractors?

Local SEO for contractors gets you shown to homeowners searching in your service area. It covers three places on the screen, each won differently.

Where you show upWhat decides itWhat you control
The map resultsThe Business Profile, reviews and distance from the searcherCategories, services, photos, reviews, replies
The regular results under the mapYour website's pages and the sites linking to themService pages, real job content, local links
AI answersWhat your reviews, website and other sites say about youThe same proof, said clearly and consistently

The map is the big one. In a 2026 WebFX test of 500 home-services searches, the local map pack showed up on 97.6% of them. If you're not in the map, you're not in most of the searches that matter.

How Google Ranks Contractors in the Map

Google tells you how it ranks the map, which is more than most agencies will. In Google's own help page: "Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity." The same page says "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking."

Google's factorWhat it means for a contractorCan you change it?
RelevanceDoes your profile match what was searched: the right category and the services listedYes, this week
DistanceHow far your business is from the person searchingNot without moving
PopularityHow well known you are: reviews, ratings, links and mentionsYes, slowly

Distance is the one nobody wants to hear about. You can't out-optimize the fact that a homeowner on the far side of the metro lives 40 minutes from your shop and 6 minutes from someone else's. Every owner has checked the map from their own driveway, seen themselves in first place and felt great. Drive twenty minutes across town and you've vanished, like a radio station in a tunnel.

So the job is to win the map where you're close, and win the regular results everywhere else. That second part is what your website is for.

Step 1: Set Up Your Google Business Profile Properly

Your Google Business Profile is the single biggest lever in local SEO for contractors, and most of it takes an afternoon. Fill every field, pick the right categories and keep it true. Here's the order I'd work through it in.

FieldWhat to do
Business nameYour real name. Google's guidelines say it "should reflect your business's real-world name." Sorry, Bob.
Primary categoryThe one that matches the job you most want, not the one that sounds biggest.
Service areaSet it honestly. Google says service-area businesses "can't list a 'virtual' office" unless it's staffed during business hours.
ServicesEvery service you sell, in the homeowner's words: composite decks, fence installation, roof replacement.
PhotosReal jobs every month: your crew, your trucks, your finished work. Stock photos fool exactly nobody.
Phone numberOne that gets answered. A profile that ranks first and goes to voicemail is a very well-optimized way to lose a job.

That last one matters more than any ranking factor. Every call your profile produces is a homeowner who picked you over the two listings next to you. Miss it and they're one tap away from picking again.

Step 2: Get a Steady Flow of Reviews

Reviews are the deciding step for most homeowners, and Google counts them for ranking too. The trick isn't a big push once a year. It's a few new reviews every month, because homeowners now ignore old ones.

Consumers who...2026
Use reviews to guide what they buy locally97%
Only care about reviews written in the last three months74%
Will only use a business rated 4 stars or higher68%, up from 55%
Won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews47%
Expect business owners to respond to reviews89%

Source: BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey 2026.

That 74% is the one that changes how you work. Fifty glowing reviews from 2022 now read like a yearbook. Homeowners want to know you're still good this year, with this crew.

The simplest system that works: ask at the final walkthrough, while the homeowner is standing on the finished deck and feeling great about it. Then send a text with the review link that evening. Reply to every review, good or bad, in a sentence or two that sounds like a person wrote it.

Even top-ranked contractors slip here. A 2024 study of 9,782 contractor profiles by Contractor Lead Partners found 8% of the companies ranking in the top 20 had a rating below 4.0 stars. They rank, and then they lose the homeowner at the stars.

Step 3: A Website That Ranks Outside the Map

Your website is how you rank beyond the few miles the map gives you. The map is tied to distance. The regular results underneath are tied to your pages, so a strong page for each service can rank across your whole service area.

Website problemShare of contractors, 2024
No website at all14%
Homepage missing its main heading tag22%
Fewer than 200 words of text on the homepage17%

Source: Contractor Lead Partners, 2024 State of Local SEO for Contractors, 9,782 profiles.

Those numbers are good news if you're on the right side of them. Clearing that bar already puts you ahead of a real slice of your competition.

Build one page per service you want to sell: deck building, deck replacement, covered patios. Each one should show real jobs, real photos, what the project involves and what homeowners usually ask. Then link your Business Profile's services to those pages.

City pages come last, and only where you have real local material. A page that swaps "Kalispell" for "Whitefish" and changes nothing else is the kind Google tends to leave unindexed. Write about the problem in that place instead: the permits, the weather, the jobs you've done three streets over.

Local SEO in a Small Market

Josiah W., a deck builder in Montana's Flathead Valley, on growing in a market of about 90,000 people.

In a small market, local SEO is one of the best deals in marketing. There are fewer competitors, the distance factor works for you, and the bar to rank first is often a profile someone actually filled in.

In a metro of three million, you'll win the map near your shop and almost nowhere else, so the website and reviews carry the rest.

Small markets get told the wrong thing all the time. Josiah W. was told flat out not to run ads in a market of about 90,000 people. He went on to get 40 to 50 leads a month through our appointment system.

A small market isn't a dead market. It's a market where fewer people are competing for the same homeowner, and that's true in search too.

Local SEO and AI Search

AI tools now recommend contractors, and they draw on the same signals local SEO builds: your reviews, your website and what other sites say about you. Doing local SEO well is most of the work of showing up in AI answers.

The shift has been fast. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 45% of consumers now use AI tools for local business recommendations, up from 6% the year before. I think an AI recommendation is the closest thing you'll ever get to another kind of referral. The homeowner asked and got your name back, no ad attached.

There's a full playbook on getting ChatGPT to recommend your contracting business. The short version: make your proof easy to find and say it the same way everywhere.

How Long Local SEO Takes, and What to Measure

Profile and review fixes can move your map rankings within weeks. Website pages and links usually take 6 to 12 months to compound. Judge it on calls and signed jobs from search, not on a ranking report.

MeasureWhere to find itWhy it matters
Calls from your profileBusiness Profile performanceThe most direct sign the map is working
Website forms from searchYour site's analytics or form toolShows your pages are pulling their weight
New reviews this monthYour profileRecency is what homeowners now check
Signed jobs from searchAsk every customer, log it in your CRMThe only number that pays a crew

SEO is a long-term asset, not a quick fix. Done right, it compounds: every page and every review keeps working next year without a click bill. The catch is that it does very little for next week's calendar, which is why I'd never make it the only thing a contractor does.

What We See in the Field

Across the 100+ contractor businesses I've worked with, a few patterns show up again and again.

  1. The phone is the ranking factor nobody tracks. Owners spend months climbing the map, then send the calls to a voicemail box that's been full since spring.
  2. Old reviews stop working. The companies that win keep asking every month, not once when they remember.
  3. Real photos beat everything else on the profile. Homeowners want to see your crew and your work, not a stock photo of a man pointing at a blueprint.
  4. Small markets are the easiest maps to own. Fewer competitors and shorter distances mean a complete profile goes a long way.
  5. SEO pays off most when something else fills the calendar first. The owners who stick with it for a year aren't panicking about next month. More on that in SEO for contractors: does it actually work?

Common Questions

How long does local SEO take for a contractor?

Profile and review fixes can move map rankings within weeks. Website pages and links usually take 6 to 12 months to compound. Judge it on calls and signed jobs from search, not on a ranking report.

Can a contractor rank in towns where they don't have an office?

In the map results, it's hard, because Google weighs how far your business is from the person searching. In the regular search results below the map, yes: a strong service page with real jobs from that area can rank well beyond your address.

What matters more for local SEO, reviews or the website?

Reviews decide whether you get picked, and the website decides whether you rank outside the map. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.

Should I put keywords in my Google Business Profile name?

No. Google's guidelines say your profile name should reflect your business's real-world name, as used on your signage, website and paperwork. Put your services and service area in the fields built for them.

Do I need a separate page for every city I serve?

Only for cities where you have real local material: jobs you've done there, local photos, local reviews. A page that only swaps the city name is the kind Google tends not to index.
