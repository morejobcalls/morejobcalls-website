The Problem

Why Landscaping Marketing Breaks for Design-Build Companies

If you sell $20K–$100K outdoor projects, your marketing problem looks nothing like a lawn company's. But every lead source treats you like one. You list on a marketplace as "landscaping" and the inbox fills with mulch refreshes, spring cleanups, and someone who wants a quote on mowing a half acre. The platforms can't tell a $50 visit from a $60K backyard, so you pay to sort through both.

Then there's the "free landscaping quote" trap. A homeowner with a Pinterest board and a $3,000 budget books a consult, you or your designer burn two hours walking the property and sketching ideas, and the conversation dies the moment you say what a paver patio actually costs. Design time is your most expensive asset and most landscape companies hand it out free to people who were never buyers.

Stack the seasonality on top. Spring buries you in inquiries you can't get to. By November the phone is quiet and the crews you fought to hire are watching the clock. Look — the problem isn't demand. The high-end backyard buyer exists in your market all year. The problem is that nothing in your current marketing finds that specific buyer, screens out everyone else, and puts them on your calendar as an exclusive appointment.

What Leads Cost

Exclusive Landscaping Leads: What They Cost Per Booked Consult

Published prices put exclusive landscape design and install leads at $50 to $150 each, exclusive mowing leads at $15 to $40, and shared leads of any type at $8 to $25. Those ranges come from a 2026 landscaping lead generation benchmark guide, and they're already more than most vendors selling the word exclusive will print anywhere on the page. Their benchmarks are labeled as 2024 to 2026 market conditions rather than tied to a published dataset, so read them for shape and not as a quote. One that does print a number lists $33.99 for an exclusive landscaping lead in Oregon, and $55 to $172 a lead through Google Ads, updated October 2026.

Here's the part none of those pages carry through to the end. That same guide puts lead-to-customer conversion on design and install work at 8% to 15%, the lowest of any landscaping service it lists, because the ticket is the biggest and the decision takes weeks. Run $50 to $150 a lead through that rate and one signed project has cost you between $333 and $1,875 in lead fees. None of that counts your designer's hours on the projects that never got funded.

Then there's the mix problem, and landscaping has it worse than most trades I look at. The same price buys you the homeowner planning a full backyard build and the one who wants a number on mulch. Your invoice can't tell them apart, so your real cost per design conversation is whatever you paid divided by the share of what you bought that was project scope in the first place.

The System

How the Exclusive Landscaping Lead System Works

Not "we'll run your Facebook ads." A complete acquisition system, installed in your accounts, built around the design-build buyer in six pieces:

  1. Marketing Autopsy & Recovery Plan. We dig through everything you've spent money on — marketplaces, SEO retainers, boosted posts — and document why each one failed to produce project-grade leads. You keep the write-up whether you hire us or not.
  2. 7-Day Launch Sprint. A qualification funnel goes live that asks about project type, budget range, property, and timeline before anyone can book. The $3K-patio crowd filters itself out at this step, before your designer's time is on the line.
  3. The Neighborhood Authority Engine. Ad creative built on your transformation photos — the before/after backyard, the finished outdoor kitchen — aimed at the affluent zip codes where your best projects already are. Leads arrive feeling like referrals, not strangers comparing three bids.
  4. Lead Handling War Room. A week-one session with you and whoever answers the phone: speed-to-lead rules, a consult-booking script, and daily targets. High-intent homeowners go cold in hours, not weeks. This fixes that.
  5. Calendar Max-Out Kit. SMS and email automations in your CRM that confirm consults, recover no-shows, and nurture the "we're planning for next season" crowd until they're ready. Off-season inquiries stop leaking away.
  6. Pipeline Pulse Reporting. One simple weekly report: opportunities in, consults booked, projects sold. You see whether the machine is working without logging into Ads Manager once.

Is an exclusive landscaping lead the same as an exclusive appointment?

No, and the gap between the two is where most of the money goes. Exclusive tells you nobody else bought that homeowner's details. It doesn't tell you whether you'll ever reach them, whether they've got a project budget, or whether they'll be home when your designer pulls up. An exclusive appointment is a homeowner who has already answered the project type, budget range, property and timeline questions and then picked a slot on your calendar. Nobody selling leads by the lead will quote you a reach rate, because that part becomes your problem the moment the invoice clears.

Marketplaces blast the same homeowner request to several companies at once, and in landscaping most of those requests are low-ticket anyway: cleanups, mulch, mowing.
