The Problem

Why Most HVAC Leads Fill the Wrong Calendar

An HVAC company is really two businesses sharing one phone number. The service side runs on emergencies: no heat in January, no cooling in July, and the homeowner hires whoever answers first. The replacement side is a planned sale. It's a system the family will live with for years, and the decision usually gets made at a kitchen table with both spouses in the room.

Most lead sources blur the two. Shared lead sites sell the same "my AC stopped working" request to several companies at once, and most of those requests are repairs. Your comfort advisor drives across town for a replacement estimate and finds a homeowner who wanted a capacitor swapped by dinner.

Then the shoulder seasons arrive. Spring and fall go quiet while payroll stays exactly the same, and the installers you hired for July spend October in the shop. There are only so many times a crew can reorganize the van.

The problem is rarely lead volume. It's that nobody separates the replacement buyer from the repair caller before the appointment gets booked. Fix that, and the same ad dollars build a different business.

The System

How the HVAC Appointment System Works

This isn't ad management. It's an appointment engine installed inside your business, in your own accounts, in six pieces:

  1. Agency Audit & Recovery Plan. We pull apart what you've spent so far: lead-site invoices, old ad accounts and the agency report nobody could explain. Then we put in writing why it didn't work and what changes. You keep the document either way.
  2. 7-Day Launch Sprint. A qualification funnel built for HVAC replacements. It asks about the system's age, repair or replace, the equipment they're considering, home ownership, timeline, financing interest and whether both decision-makers can attend. It's wired into your CRM with tracking from day one.
  3. The Neighborhood Authority Engine. Video of you and your comfort advisors explaining replacements in plain English: when a repair stops making sense, what a heat pump changes and what the install day looks like. It reaches homeowners whose system is getting old before it fails, while they're still comparing options instead of panicking.
  4. Lead Handling War Room. A week-one session with your owner, sales team and office: speed-to-lead rules, repair-or-replace scripts and how to book so both spouses are at the table.
  5. Sales Calendar Max-Out Kit. Routing rules, text and email confirmations that re-ask the both-spouses question, no-show recovery and reactivation flows. Repair-only inquiries go to your service board instead of clogging the sales calendar.
  6. Pipeline Pulse Reporting. A weekly report with the three numbers that matter: new opportunities, appointments set and jobs sold. You check math, not vibes.

HVAC Marketing — Common Questions

Do you market HVAC emergency repair calls?

No. We book planned replacement and install appointments: furnaces, air conditioners, heat pumps and ductless systems. Emergency repair is a speed business, and other agencies do it well. Inquiries that turn out to be repairs go to your service team instead of the sales calendar.

Lead companies sell the same homeowner to several HVAC contractors and let you race each other to the phone. We run the ads inside your own accounts, so every appointment is yours alone and never resold. You own the ad account and your CRM data from day one.
