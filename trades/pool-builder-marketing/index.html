The Problem

Why Most Pool Builder Marketing Wastes Your Design Consults

Pools have a problem no other trade has at this scale: the gap between who clicks and who can buy. Your real customer is signing for $60K to $150K or more, often with a financing conversation in the middle and months between first call and contract. But the internet doesn't sort by budget. "Free quote" portals and shared lead sites hand you a list where the $120K inground buyer sits right next to the guy pricing a $5,000 above-ground kit, and you can't tell them apart until you've burned an afternoon at the kitchen table.

That's the expensive part. A wasted lead in roofing costs a phone call. A wasted lead in pools costs a design consultation: site visit, drawings, sometimes a 3D render, hours of your best closer's time. Do that for tire-kickers all spring and you've paid for marketing twice — once in fees, once in payroll.

Here's the thing: you don't have a lead volume problem. You have a sorting problem. Fix the sorting and the same ad spend produces a calendar full of homeowners who own the property, know the ballpark, and are ready to talk financing.

The System

How the Pool Builder Marketing System Works

This isn't "we'll run your Facebook ads." It's a six-piece operating system installed inside your own accounts, tuned for high-ticket backyard projects:

  1. Agency Audit & Recovery Plan. Before a dollar of ad spend moves, we audit what you've already tried — portals, SEO contracts, the last ads guy — and put in writing why it didn't produce signed contracts. You keep the document either way.
  2. 7-Day Launch Sprint. A multi-step funnel built around the pool buyer: project type, budget range, financing readiness, property ownership, timeline. The questions that protect your design consults get asked before the booking, not after.
  3. The Neighborhood Authority Engine. Proof-heavy creative that reaches homeowners while they're still in the dreaming-and-planning stage — fall and winter included — so you're selling next summer's build before your competitors wake up in March.
  4. Lead Handling War Room. A week-one session with your owner and sales side to set speed-to-lead rules and consult standards. Pool buying cycles are long; the builder who responds first and follows up longest wins the contract.
  5. Sales Calendar Max-Out Kit. SMS and email automation built for a long consideration cycle: nurture for the planners, reminders for the booked, recovery for the no-shows. A homeowner who isn't ready in October stays warm until they are.
  6. Pipeline Pulse Reporting. A weekly report with the only numbers that matter: qualified opportunities in, consults booked, contracts signed. No impression charts.
