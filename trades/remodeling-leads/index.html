The Problem

Why Most Remodeling Leads Never Turn Into Projects

Most remodeling leads are sold the moment a homeowner fills out a form. Shared lead sites send the same kitchen to four or five remodelers, and the homeowner picks whoever answers first or bids lowest. Pay-per-lead sellers promise exclusive leads, but a lead is still only a form fill. Nobody has asked about the budget yet.

So you drive 40 minutes to measure a bathroom for someone who, it turns out, wanted to know what tile costs. Per tile. You smile and say you'll send something over, and you both know you won't.

The remodelers who grow fastest don't buy more leads. They screen for scope and budget before the appointment, then follow up for as long as the design phase takes. That's the part I build for you.

The System

How the Remodeling Appointment System Works

You don't buy leads from us. We install an appointment engine inside your business, in your own accounts, in six pieces:

  1. Agency Audit & Recovery Plan. We go through what you've already spent: lead-site invoices, the Houzz subscription, old ad accounts and the website that looks great and never rings. Then we put in writing what went wrong and what changes. You keep the document either way.
  2. 7-Day Launch Sprint. A qualification funnel built for remodeling. It asks which rooms are involved, the rough budget, the timeline, whether a designer or financing is part of it, home ownership and whether both decision-makers can attend. It's wired into your CRM with tracking from day one.
  3. The Neighborhood Authority Engine. Video of you walking finished kitchens and baths and explaining how a remodel actually runs: the design choices, the lead times on cabinets and what happens when the old subfloor turns out to be soft. It reaches homeowners while they're still saving photos, before they fill out a form that gets sold to five other companies.
  4. Lead Handling War Room. A week-one session with your owner, designer and office: speed-to-lead rules, budget conversations that don't scare people off and how to book so both spouses are at the table.
  5. Sales Calendar Max-Out Kit. Routing rules, text and email confirmations, no-show recovery and follow-up that keeps open bids warm through the design phase. Small-job requests get a polite no instead of a site visit.
  6. Pipeline Pulse Reporting. A weekly report with the three numbers that matter: new opportunities, appointments set and jobs sold. You check math, not vibes.

Remodeling sells on trust, and trust takes a few meetings. That's why the follow-up matters as much as the ads. If you also take on additions and custom builds, the general contractor page covers those projects.

FAQ

Remodeling Leads — Common Questions

What's the difference between a remodeling lead and a booked appointment?

A remodeling lead is a name and a phone number from someone who filled out a form. A booked appointment is a homeowner who has already answered questions about the project, the budget and the timeline, and has picked a time to meet you. We deliver appointments, and every one is yours alone. We never share or resell them.

Which remodeling projects do you book?

Kitchen remodels, bathroom remodels and whole-home renovations, plus basements and other interior projects big enough to need a sales appointment. The funnel screens out handyman work, single-fixture swaps and people who are only collecting prices.

What results have you gotten for remodelers?
