The Problem

Why Most Deck Builder Marketing Fails

If you run a deck company, you've probably lived some version of this: five-star work, awards on the wall, crews that know what they're doing — and a January calendar that's dead quiet. Feast-or-famine isn't a marketing problem you have. It's the marketing problem in this trade.

And the usual fixes make it worse. Shared lead marketplaces sell the same homeowner to three or four deck builders at once, so whoever quotes cheapest and calls fastest wins. SEO agencies sell you a 12-month contract and traffic reports with a million impressions and nothing you can attribute. Most deck builders we talk to have sunk $20K–$70K into this stuff before they ever find us — and most have been burned by six or seven agencies.

Here's the thing: the problem isn't that ads don't work for deck builders. It's that leads aren't the product you actually need. Appointments are.

The System

How the Deck Builder Marketing System Works

This isn't "ad management." It's an operating system installed inside your business, in your own accounts, in six pieces:

  1. Agency Audit & Recovery Plan. Before any ads go live, we audit everything you've tried and put in writing why it failed and what changes. You keep this even if you never hire us.
  2. 7-Day Launch Sprint. A one-page, multi-step qualification funnel, targeting tuned to your best zip codes and project types, and tracking wired into your CRM.
  3. The Neighborhood Authority Engine. Proof-heavy creative that catches homeowners before they start price-shopping — so leads arrive feeling like referrals, not cold inquiries three other builders are chasing.
  4. Lead Handling War Room. A week-one working session with your owner, sales, and admin to install scripts, speed-to-lead rules, and daily targets.
  5. Sales Calendar Max-Out Kit. Routing rules, SMS and email sequences, no-show recovery, and reactivation automations inside your CRM. Non-responders get nurtured instead of lost.
  6. Pipeline Pulse Reporting. A simple weekly report: new opportunities, appointments set, jobs sold. Numbers you can check, not vibes.
