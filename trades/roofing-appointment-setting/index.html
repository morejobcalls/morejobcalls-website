Key Takeaway

Roofing appointment setting is everything between "a homeowner raised a hand" and "a roofer is on their porch". You can pay a person to do it (an in-house setter, a call center or a canvasser), pay a vendor per appointment, or install a system that does it automatically inside your own accounts.

The booking is the easy half. The appointment only pays if the homeowner is serious and shows up, so judge every option on who it books and how many of them are home when you knock.

Show Rate

Roofing Appointments Only Count If the Homeowner Is Home

A booked inspection and a run inspection are two different numbers, and the gap between them is where appointment setting quietly fails. The fix that has the most research behind it is boring: remind people.

Who sends the reminder?What usually happens
In-house setterWhatever they remember to do between new calls
Call centerOnly if it's in the contract, and often not after hours
CanvasserRarely. They're already three streets over
Pay-per-appointment vendorDepends on whether you pay per booked or per held
The More Jobs SystemEvery time: confirmation, reminders before the visit, and a no-show recovery sequence offering a new time

A meta-analysis in Health Services Research pooled 18 studies of text reminders. In the randomized trials, people who got a reminder had about 1.5 times the odds of showing up.

The wording matters too. A study of 161,587 hospital patients in Israel tested different reminder texts. The best-worded one cut no-shows from 21.1% to 14.2% against the generic message.

Those are clinics, not roofs. The habit is the same though. Anybody who booked something on a Monday and forgot by Thursday knows it, and a homeowner with a slow leak is no different from a patient with a sore knee. Neither one wakes up thinking about you.

Roofing Appointment Setting: Common Questions

What is roofing appointment setting?

Roofing appointment setting is the work between a homeowner showing interest and a roofer standing in their driveway: reaching the homeowner, checking the project is real, booking a time and making sure they're home for it. It can be done by an in-house setter, an outsourced call center, door-knocking canvassers, a pay-per-appointment vendor, or an automated system like ours.

How much does a roofing appointment setter cost?

An in-house setter is usually paid like a customer service rep, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics puts that median at $21.53 an hour, or $44,770 a year, before payroll taxes and training. Outsourced setters start lower: one roofing virtual assistant company advertises rates from $15 an hour, and canvassers average $22.10 an hour on Indeed's salary data. We don't publish our price, and you get the real number for your market on a 30-minute strategy call.

Are there companies that book sales appointments for roofers?

Yes. Some call the leads you already have, some sell you appointments one at a time, and some, like MoreJobCalls.com, build the whole system that brings the homeowner in and books them. Ask any of them who else gets that homeowner. Every appointment we book is exclusive to your company.

Should I hire an in-house roofing appointment setter or outsource it?

Hire in-house if you have steady lead flow and someone to manage a setter daily. Outsource if calling is the bottleneck but the leads are good. If there aren't enough serious homeowners to call, a setter of either kind just reaches the same thin list faster.

Has this worked for other roofers?

Yes. David L., a roofing contractor in North Carolina, closed 17 deals from January through March and confirmed on a recorded call that it came to $65,000 in profit on $12,000 of ad spend. Another roofing client had 12 of their 20 June roofs from the system by the 19th.

Why do roofing appointments no-show, and do reminders help?

Homeowners forget, get a cheaper call first, or never felt committed when they booked. Reminders fix the forgetting. A meta-analysis in Health Services Research found that in randomized trials, people who got a text reminder had about 1.5 times the odds of showing up. Every appointment we book gets confirmation and reminder texts and emails, and a no-show gets a recovery sequence offering a new time.

Do you knock doors or chase storms?

No. The system brings in homeowners who asked about a roof, in your own service area, and books them onto your calendar. It works for retail replacements and fills the gaps between storms if you also do insurance work.
