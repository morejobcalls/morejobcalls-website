The Problem

Why Window Replacement Leads Are Harder Than They Should Be

The window industry did this to itself. Decades of high-pressure one-call closes, "buy 2 get 2 free" pricing theater, and tonight-only discounts taught homeowners one lesson: when a window company calls, put your guard up. So the homeowner who fills out a form online expects a hard sell before your rep ever knocks. That's the trust gap every window company inherits, whether you sell that way or not.

Then the lead itself is broken. Marketplaces resell the same window lead to four or five companies. Within an hour the homeowner has six missed calls, stops answering entirely, and the job goes to whoever discounted hardest. And when someone does sit an appointment, half the time it's a one-legger. One spouse at the table, no decision possible, demo wasted, close rate wrecked. You paid for that appointment three times: the lead cost, the rep's evening, and the follow-up that never lands.

Here's the thing. Windows are an $8K–$30K researched purchase. Homeowners spend weeks comparing brands, ratings, and financing before they talk to anyone. The company that shows up as the credible local authority during that research, instead of the sixth voicemail after a form fill, wins the appointment before price ever comes up.

What Leads Cost

Window Leads for Sale: What You Pay Per Appointment That Sits

Published prices for window leads run about $70 for a raw form fill, about $140 once the seller has verified it, and at least $160 for a lead sold to you alone. Those are Fixr's own published numbers, and the same page says it takes about three verified leads to set one appointment, so you pay 3x. That means the $160 exclusive lead is really $480 by the time one appointment is on the calendar, before anybody has checked whether both owners will be home.

Then the appointment still has to happen. Write-ups on bought window leads put the share of set appointments that actually get run at 45% to 65%, which is a polite way of saying a third to a half of what you paid for never gets in front of a rep. A no-show costs you the same as a demo you got to give. So does the reschedule that quietly never happens, and so does the one where only one spouse is home.

So the honest comparison between any two sellers isn't cost per lead. It's cost per appointment your rep gets to sit, with both decision-makers at the table.

Should I buy window leads?

You can, and plenty of window contractors do. Just know what's in the box. Most window leads for sale are form fills resold to several companies, so you're paying to enter a race, not to meet a homeowner. Before you buy window replacement leads from anyone, ask three things: how many other companies get the same homeowner, who calls them in the first hour, and whether both decision-makers are confirmed. If the answers are "several," "you" and "no," the price per lead is the smallest number you'll pay. Should I run PPC for window replacement or social ads?
